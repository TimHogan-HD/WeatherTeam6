Crag Score Math

Every crag's score answers two questions for each hour: is the rock dry, and will it stick? Each answer is a number from 0 to 1. Multiply them, times 100, and that is the score.

Score=100×Dryness×Grip

The app asks how dry the rock is and how good the friction is, each out of 100%, and multiplies them. Dry rock in sweaty heat scores low. Perfect temps on soaked rock scores low. You need both.

Why multiply, not average

Multiplying means either factor alone can sink the score. Averaging would let a perfect, crisp day hide a wet wall. Drag the two sliders.

The whole pipeline

From forecast to number

Every hour, for the next 7 days, plus 7 days of past weather so the app knows when it last rained.

Factor 1

Dryness: the drying clock

Each rock type has a drying window: how many hours a full soak (0.4 inches of rain or more) takes to dry. Granite is 6 hours. Soft sandstone is 120. The clock tracks one number, the hours of drying still needed.

  • Rain adds hours. Smaller rain needs less: a tenth of an inch needs half the window, not a quarter (it scales with the square root of the rain).
  • Each dry hour subtracts at least one hour. Sun, wind and dry air can make an hour count for up to 3. Rock that gets fragile when wet (soft sandstone, porous limestone, unknown) always dries at exactly 1 hour per hour.
  • Dryness is the share of the window that has dried, squared. Squaring makes it climb slowly at first: half dried scores 25, three quarters 56. Rock strength comes back late.
  • Rain falling hard enough to see (0.02 in/h or more) scores the hour 0, whatever the storm size.

Fully dry after

Every rock has a drying time after a real soak. A small shower uses up part of it. Each dry hour knocks at least an hour off, more in sun and wind. The score stays low until most of that time has passed, because wet rock is weak and greasy longer than the surface looks.

Rain on top of rain, snow, and the first hour
  • New rain adds to the water still in the rock. A shower after a soak never resets the clock to zero, and never makes it longer than a fresh soak of the combined rain.
  • Rain at or below 33 °F builds up as snow. Melt re-wets the rock, and while 1 mm of snow water lies, dryness is capped at 0.25.
  • The very first hour of history is treated as soaked, because the rain before it is unknown. That is the safe direction to be wrong.
  • The app imagines eight walls facing every compass direction at the crag and takes the middle one each hour.
Factor 2

Grip: four penalties, multiplied

Grip starts at 100% and four things can take it down. Each fades smoothly past its edge. None can flip on suddenly.

  • Heat: above 60 °F air, grip fades. About 21 °F past the edge it is down to 37%.
  • Cold: below 30 °F, the same fade. Both edges move if you set your own range in Profile.
  • Humidity: dew point above 54 °F (sticky air) fades grip. 18 °F past that, it is at 37%.
  • Condensation: if the rock is near the dew point, it sweats. The rock's temperature is its slow memory of the last two days of air, plus the sun warming each wall. Clear once the rock is 3.6 °F (2 °C) above the dew point. It can never take more than half the score.

Grip goes down when it's hot, freezing, muggy, or when the rock is cold enough that moisture condenses on it. The app multiplies those together. The Score breakdown tells you which one is costing the most.

From hours to days

How a day gets one number

The day's score is the worst hour of the day's best 3-hour run between 8 am and 6 pm (your climbing hours, adjustable in Profile). One lucky mild hour cannot carry a hot day, and one wet morning does not ruin a good afternoon. An hour with no data breaks the run.

A day's number is the worst hour in the best three-hour block you could climb. So a 70 means you get at least three straight hours at 70 or better.

When there's no number

A blank beats a guess

  • If any input is missing (rain not measured, no sun reading, too little history), the hour has no score, not a made-up one.
  • After an hour of unknown rain, every hour stays blank until a full drying window could have passed.
  • Weather alerts show beside the score. They never change or hide it.
  • Days 7 to 15 show weather only, no score.

If the app doesn't know, it shows a dash instead of guessing.

Reference

Drying windows by rock

"Wet until" is when the rock stops reading Wet after a full soak and starts reading Drying. "Dry after" is the full window. These are field rules and climber consensus, not measurements; nobody has measured drying time for climbing rock.
