Background options

Why A to G look alike: on a 0 to 100 lightness scale they run from 2 to 14, all of which reads as black on a phone. I, J and K sit at 18 to 23, where a dark theme starts to read as grey or colour.

Same Overview as variant C, eleven options. The page behind the phone takes the chosen ground, so it reads like a full screen. A to E move only the ground and the steps above it (tiles, the dock, hairlines); F drops the glow, G separates by tone instead of lines, and H is a light theme for a phone in light mode. Research notes are under each option's figures. Figures are a sample Taylors Falls week.

A · Current

  • Near black, #07090c, under a teal glow at the top that fades out by mid-screen. Most of the screen is the near-black.
  • Highest contrast of the five, and the reason it reads as very dark: the ground reflects about 0.3% of white.
  • On OLED phones a near-black ground can smear when scrolling (pixels switching off and on), and bright text on it can glow at the edges (halation). Material's dark theme uses #121212 rather than black for both reasons.

B · Charcoal

  • Neutral dark grey, #15181c, close to Material's #121212 recommendation. About three times the light of A.
  • Plain and calm. It drops the blue-black tint, so the teal brand and the lime scores carry all the colour.
  • The glow at the top is nearly gone; the ground is close to flat.

C · Slate

  • Blue-grey, #161d26. Keeps today's cool tint, lifted to about four times A's light.
  • Reads as night sky rather than black. The rain blue sits closer to the ground, so rain figures stand out a little less than on B or D.

D · Deep teal

  • Dark teal, #0f1d21, with the top glow kept. About four times A's light.
  • The ground takes the brand hue, so the app has one identity instead of black plus an accent. The lime scores and amber and red still separate clearly from it.
  • My pick: it answers "too dark" without going grey, and it keeps the one thing in the current look that was chosen for this app (the teal glow).

E · Dim

  • Lifted slate, #1d242d, about six times A's light. The softest of the five; closest to a "dimmed" theme.
  • Hairlines and tiles need bigger steps to show, and the red score on a tile drops to 4.9:1, the nearest to the floor of any option.
  • In direct sun on the crag, a lighter dark ground loses some punch. Size and contrast carry sunlight readability, not darkness.

F · Teal, flat

  • D without the glow. The radial glow tells the reader nothing; it is the "atmospheric gradient" #637 lists among the tells of a generated look. A flat ground puts the eye on the score instead.
  • Compare with D: if you can't tell which one you prefer, the glow isn't earning its place.
  • Removing it also removes one token (glass.groundGlow) and the gradient from globals.css.

G · Tonal steps

  • Material 3's approach: a neutral ground (#121416) and a fixed ladder of lighter surfaces, one step per level. A lighter surface means "closer to you", so the tiles separate by tone and the hairlines can fade.
  • Follows two research rules: a surface ladder with one job per step (Radix's 12-step scale), and group by space and tone before borders (NN/g: borders are "often added in an abundance of caution").
  • Most legible of the dark options: body text 17:1, inactive tab 5.1:1 on a tile. Apple asks dark mode to "strive for 7:1, especially in small text"; muted text here is 11:1.
  • Bigger than B to E: the hairlines between rows go nearly invisible, so the lists rely on spacing alone. Worth checking on your phone.

I · Graphite

  • #262c33, lightness 18 of 100 (A is 2, E is 14). The first step that reads as grey rather than black on a phone.
  • Every colour stays as built and clears 4.5:1: body text 13:1, lime 10.9:1, red on a tile 4.6:1.

J · Storm

  • #2e3540, lightness 22. Blue-grey, close to Twitter/X's "Dim" theme lifted a step. Clearly a dark theme, clearly not black.
  • The red score lightens to #ffa0a5 so small red text keeps 4.5:1 on a tile (today's red would be 4.0:1). Lime is 9.5:1, body text 11.5:1.
  • The limit: much lighter than this and the light text starts losing contrast faster than it gains comfort.

K · Teal, mid

  • #1e3a40, lightness 23. D's brand teal at J's lightness: the app becomes a teal app rather than a black one.
  • Same red lift as J. Lime on teal is 9.3:1, but the brand teal accents (selected day, dock pill) sit closer to the ground, so the selection reads more by its outline than its fill.

H · Light mode

  • A second theme, not a replacement. The app would follow the phone's own light or dark setting, never an in-app switch (Apple: "Avoid offering an app-specific appearance setting").
  • Why consider it: in dim light, dark text on a light ground reads faster than the reverse (Dobres et al. 2017); in daylight the two did not differ. NN/g advises against dark-only for a general audience.
  • The cost: the score colours change shade. Lime on white measures about 1.3:1, so "good" becomes a dark green (#3b6a00, 5.3:1 on a tile), and amber and red darken too. Every chart's data ramps need a light version.
  • Sizeable: a full second token set and a check:ui pass in each mode. It could follow any of A to G as the dark half.

What B to G change: the ground, the glow, the surface, tile and hairline steps, and the inactive tab grey (lifted so it stays over 4.5:1 on a tile; today's measures 4.2:1 there). The phone's status bar colour follows the ground. Text, scores and the brand teal are untouched and stay well over the 4.5:1 floor. H also changes the text and score shades, as its note says.

Cost: B to G are about seven token values in packages/design and a check:ui pass; no layout or data change. H is a second theme.

The options combine. Ground (B, C, D, E or G), glow or flat (F), and light mode (H) are three separate choices: for example "D, flat, no light mode yet".

What the research says

  • Not pure black. Material's dark theme recommends a dark grey (#121212) over black: it shows elevation and avoids the smear and edge glow bright text gets on OLED black.
  • One job per surface step. Radix: ground, subtle background, element, hover, selected, borders, then text. Today's ladder has a ground, a surface and a tile that do overlapping jobs.
  • Fewer colours, each with a meaning. NN/g: "Limit your palette to three colors", about 60/30/10. A neutral or brand-tinted ground leaves the lime, amber and red as the only strong colours, so they read as scores.
  • Decoration should carry information. Glass on content cards and atmospheric gradients are tells of a generated look (#637); Apple: "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer."
  • Sunlight is about size and contrast, not darkness: polarity made no difference in daylight (Dobres et al. 2017). That is why every option keeps text above 4.5:1 rather than chasing the darkest ground.
