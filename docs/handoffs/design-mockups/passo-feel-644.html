A crag, lit · v3

Red Wing on Wednesday at 9 AM, 14 hours after Monday night's rain. The sliders above the phone are live. Ground runs from Passo's near-black at 4% up to a lifted charcoal at 18%.

A brighter dark

  • The ground is now a lifted charcoal (11% lightness) instead of near-black, and each page carries a trace of its own hue: neutral on Today and Days, blue-grey on Rain, warm on Rock.
  • Cards are raised objects. They are lighter than the ground, a hair lighter at the top than the bottom, and have two soft shadows tinted with the ground's hue (the library's three depth levels).
  • A fine moving grain sits over everything, so the ground reads as a surface rather than a flat RGB fill. It has its own slider.

Sunrise is now the Tick dial (v16)

Search live: the crag switcher (v11)

  • Tap the crag's name, or the magnifier beside it. The library's Search live opens over the page: no box and no dropdown, just a line of big type on a rule.
  • Before you type, your crags are listed small. Type and the best match swells to headline size while the others shrink under it, with your letters lit inside every one. Each keystroke re-ranks and the lines spring to new places. Matching is loose ("tyf" finds Taylors Falls), and your saved crags win a tie.
  • The big line carries its meta: the score now (in its score colour, the only colour here) and the rock. A crag you have not saved says "not saved". Arrow down, or run a finger over the list, and the big line travels like a lens. Enter or a tap opens it: the return key flips to a tick, then the sheet closes.
  • Nothing found: the magnifier shakes its head and a line says so.
  • Sound, as the library's: a tick when the best match changes, a finer one as you move, two rising notes on open, a low thud on no match. The same Sound switch.
  • Only Red Wing is drawn, so opening another crag closes the sheet without switching. The self-typing demo is left out: this is an app, not a showcase. The other scores are placeholders.

Further out: the Dusk strip (v8, bottom of Days)

  • The library's Dusk strip is a view toward the horizon: near things sharp, far ones in haze. Here the distance is days. The strip runs 16 days from today, the length of the ensemble outlook trips already use.
  • The haze is data, not a control. It starts where the scores stop (7 days, the readings' window), because past that a day is weather only and fewer forecasts reach each day. It thickens with distance, so day 8 is readable and day 16 barely is.
  • A trip is a place on the horizon. Dec 5–6 sits in the haze, blurred. Each day it gets closer the haze recedes, and the trip sharpens once it falls inside the scores' reach. That is the app's main job, trip planning with confidence that improves over time, made visible.
  • It ticks, as the library's does: a fine tick for each day under your finger, pitched higher the further out, and a lower, longer note when you cross the scores' reach or step onto or off the trip. It stays silent until you first touch the page, and there is a Sound switch in the panel above the phone.
  • Drag or tap the strip to pick a day: score (a dash past the reach), low to high, rain chance. The thin white handle is your pick, not the visibility.
  • In the mockup the haze is a fixed gradient. Built for real, its thickness per day would come from how far apart the forecasts are, which still needs a measure and the owner's sign-off.

Hours, rebuilt to match (v7)

  • v6 was a toolbar and a table: three rows of chips in a sticky grey bar, then ruled rows edge to edge. Nothing else on the site looks like that, which is why it broke the theme.
  • Now it is built like Days. The lit object at the top is a strip of 18 hour capsules, with height as the score and the chosen hour lit, glowing and rippling. Tap it or drag along it. A hatched bracket under it marks the good hours.
  • The hour is a raised card with its score as the big rolling figure and nine labelled figures under it. This replaces the old header score and the row that expanded.
  • Every hour sits below in its own card. The reading picker is one segmented pill, and List / Chart is a pair of icons. The rows have no rules between them, and the chosen hour is a soft pill. Tapping a row picks it and scrolls back up to its card. In Chart mode the charts share the card, and dragging any of them moves the strip and the card.
  • The reading picker is the library's Tabs glide (v10). Five words and one drop of light, no bar. Choose one and the drop's front edge leaves first and the back follows, so it stretches thin, lands and wobbles still. The words turn dark exactly where it covers them. The list or charts slide in from the side you're heading to, and a small drip sounds (the same Sound switch). With a mouse, pointing at a word leans the drop toward it. Arrow keys, Home and End work.
  • Days are plain words with a dot under the chosen one, not white pills. Only Today is drawn.

Rain, after your comments

  • Last rain is the gauge. The whole card fills with water to 0.42 on a scale of 0 to 1 in, with the ticks on its right edge. The surface waves, light bands drift through the water, and it fills with a small overshoot when the page opens. The water sits behind the number (v12, after your comment): 0.42 stands whole and crisp in front of it, with a soft blue shade so it reads over the bright water.
  • Past week is by day now. Seven small gauges hold each day's total, labelled. Tap a day and its 24 hours open underneath at a size a phone can read. Monday is open to start.
  • Next 48 hours says something even when it's dry. Three figures always lead: the highest chance and when, the total, and the wettest hour. The chart under them is the chance as a line on a 0–100% scale, with amounts as bars. Switch "Next 48 h" to Dry in the panel above the phone: the chart folds to a thin strip and the third figure becomes the next likely rain from the 7-day forecast (Sat, 90%), so a dry forecast still points somewhere.

A dot with a pulsing halo rides it. Chance of rain is dots whose area matches the chance, not capsules. The past is no longer a grey box: those hours are simply undrawn grey.
