A · Current

  • Glass on every card. Apple keeps glass for the navigation layer, never content.
  • Lime means two things. It marks the scores and also the Feedback and + Add buttons, so an action wears a score colour.
  • The crag name shows twice in the first screen, and a tracked-caps eyebrow sits over every card.
  • Text down to 9 px (the hero labels, the tile labels, the source line). The floor in the skill is 11 px.
  • About eight text sizes in this one view: 92, 30, 24, 20, 19, 13, 12, 10, 9.

B · Quiet cards

  • One solid card fill, no glass, no inner highlight and no shadow. Cards hold groups (Next days, Right now); the hero sits on the ground.
  • Lime only on scores. Feedback becomes a plain text control in the muted colour, still 44 px tall.
  • Sentence-case headings replace the tracked caps. The name appears once.
  • Right now as label and value rows, not three tiles in a card.
  • Smallest text is 12 px.

C · Open list

  • No cards. Space and one hairline group things. NN/g: proximity first, a container only when space cannot do it.
  • Next days as a list, one day per row, because days are compared. NN/g: cards are a poor choice for comparison.
  • The hero is a number and one line, so the first screen reaches the week without scrolling.
  • Same colour rules and type scale as B.

Cost: larger. Next days and the hourly chart would need relayout, and the card reordering (Rearrange cards) would rearrange sections instead.

2. How sure is a day's score

Taylors Falls

Seven days

score · high/low · rain

A day's score is the worst hour of its best three-hour run inside your climbing hours.

Current

Six 100s in a row. Nothing in the row says Saturday and Sunday are less settled than Tuesday, so the score cannot rank the days a climber is choosing between.

The research (LeClerc & Joslyn 2015; Burgeno & Joslyn, WCAS): adding a probability to a single-value forecast improved decisions and trust in lab tasks. Student samples.

1 · Model dots

  • Four dots beside every score: filled for a model that leaves the rock dry, ringed in rain colour for one that leaves it wet, and the count in words.
  • Ties among 100s break by eye: Tue–Wed 4 of 4 dry, Sun 1 of 4.
  • Extends the Next days rain-split dots, which today show only when two or more models are wet.

Data: exists. ReadingsDay.rain_split (wet, models) is already on /hourly for every day scored 70 or more. Below 70 it is absent, and the row shows no dots.

2 · Split dims the score

  • The score stays lime only while the models agree. With two or more models wet it turns white and carries models split.
  • A one-line summary on top names the days the models agree on.
  • Quieter than dots; the colour change does work only alongside its words (WCAG 1.4.1).

Data: the same rain_split. Copy check: lime then means "good and agreed", which changes what the score colour means everywhere it appears. Owner call.

3 · Chance of a good day

  • The headline becomes the share of forecast runs in which the day scores 70 or more, in the same form as chance of rain. The score stays beside it.
  • This is the format the research found most useful: the chance of crossing the threshold the person decides on.
  • Plain words, no p10/p90, so it fits the copy rules.

Data: does not exist. It needs Crag A run per ensemble member for each day: about 50 runs per crag per request, or stored at collect time. A scoring-model change, and it would need an outcome check before any screen leads with it.
