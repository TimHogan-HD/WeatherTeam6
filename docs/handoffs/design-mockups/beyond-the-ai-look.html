Plate 1

The day's shape is the hero, and the number sits on it

Today: a 92 px lime "100" beside a 2×2 grid of stat tiles. This is Construct's dark-fintech face almost exactly, and L11 in avoid-ai-design ("big number as hero, fine only when the number is the product").

Proposal: the hero is the day's score as a band of hours, with night shaded and the now marker on it. The number shrinks to a label and value and sits on the band at its hour. Tide Guide (ADA 2026) and Surfline both lead with the data's own shape; Surfline's rating is a coloured bar per hour.

Why the number moves onto the band High

Kay et al. (CHI 2016, n=541 bus riders): "a too-prominent display of the point estimate causes users to ignore the probabilistic one", and the estimate and its spread "should coincide spatially". The Air chart does the same: the median line is drawn inside its p10–p90 spread, not beside it.

Linked cursor High

Apple's chart guidance (WWDC22 110340/110342, WWDC23 10037): selection draws a rule and an annotation, the touch target is the full plot height, and related charts move together. Today only the Today chart scrubs; Hourly and Day charts have no shared cursor.

Buildable now. Hourly scores, temp_c_p10/p90 and members_wet are already in GET /hourly. Rules kept: the hero still prints Score at <hour>, one score; no day score in the hero; the chart's figure is the series it draws.

Plate 2

A week of hours instead of a wall of 100s

Today: Next days, Daily and Trips print rows of identical lime 100s. A day's score is its worst hour, so a good week saturates at the ceiling and every day looks the same. The number carries no difference the climber can act on.

Proposal: each day is a strip of its hours coloured by score, on one shared time axis, so a short window and an all-day window look different at a glance. The worst-hour score stays at the right, smaller. Days where models disagree about the rock carry the split on the strip itself (hatched), not as dots beside it.

Why Medium

  • Surfline's forecast is a coloured timeline per day with night shaded; the rating is a band, not a figure.
  • beta.rocks dropped its friction score for flags (rain, wet rock, dew, wind) after users found the number "useless"; the strip keeps the score but makes its shape visible.
  • Apple: tapping a summary row highlights the matching marks. Tapping a day here lights its strip and opens one line of its readings.

Buildable now for hours inside the 7-day window. Needs your call: the hatched split moves rain_split from dots to the strip; readings.md says Daily shows no dots (2026-11-24) and Next days' dots appear only through rainSplitShown. This changes where, not when.

Plate 3

Show the forecast as it is: several futures

Today: the charts draw one line, and uncertainty appears only as text fragments ("forecasts differ") or the rain-split dots.

Proposal: draw the spread on the chart. Acme Weather, from the Dark Sky team (Feb 2026), puts light grey alternate predictions behind the forecast line on the same graph: "Our forecasts embrace uncertainty, instead of pretending it doesn't exist."

Which form High

  • Fernandes et al. (CHI 2018, n=408): quantile dotplots of 20–50 outcomes reached 97% of optimal decisions; text descriptions of uncertainty were inconsistent.
  • Kay 2016: dotplots read with about 1.15× lower variance than density plots.
  • A spread band is the cheapest honest form; C is for a tapped hour, not a whole chart.

A: buildable now from p10/p90. B: needs API work, the response carries percentiles, not member series. C: buildable as quantiles from p10/p50/p90 only by interpolation, which would invent a shape; honest dots need the member values. No score has a spread: a per-member score is new scoring work and a decision for you.

Plate 4

Evidence from your own days

Today: the Report button collects forecast checks, and whole-app.html v6 already sketched "Your checks here". Nothing on a crag screen uses them.

Proposal: on a crag, show the past checks whose forecast resembled today's, with what you found. Surfline's "Forecast matches" marks future windows like your logged sessions, and "Cam matches" shows past days with similar conditions. Flighty states causes in plain words. Both turn a number into something you have seen.

Why Medium

This is the one feature an AI-generated weather app cannot fake: it needs your history. It also answers CLAUDE.md's open problem, that Crag A is not validated against outcomes, in a place you will look.

Needs a product decision and API work. Matching needs a similarity rule over stored readings (yesterday_hours keeps one day only). The second card must never read as validation; with 11 checks it says so.

Plate 5

Colour as roles, not a bag of swatches

Use the toggle at the top. Today: lime score on near-black (SD2, the finance category colour), a cyan brand (SD8, Impeccable's "bright cyan on dark"), a glowing ring (C5), and about 20 data hues. #7fd1c4 means wind, dew margin and cool temperature at once (C8). Grade bars reuse the score lime for 5.11.

Proposal: six roles. Score keeps its three-step ladder in hues from the rock (lichen, ochre, iron) rather than acid lime. Action is ink, as v6 already proposed. Rain is one blue with steps in lightness. Temperature and wind are ink. No glow. Progress is a filled shape, not a three-segment meter.

Construct's colour guide: "refuse the category colour in writing", one accent with one role, "never a glow", "progress as a filled shape". Impeccable: build roles, not swatches; colour is never the only code. The hues are a first pass from the subject (sandstone, lichen, iron staining) and need your eye on a phone in daylight; each clears 6.6:1 on the card (WCAG contrast, computed).

What this touches

  • Owner rules kept: one score per hero, named Score at <hour>; words from summarizeReadings, never from the number; a span's score is its worst hour; gaps print as gaps.
  • Needs your call: plate 2 moves the rain split onto the strip; plate 4 is a new feature; plate 5 replaces the score hues you adopted in variant C (2026-11-23).
  • Not proposed: word ratings like "Prime" drawn from the score. readings.md forbids words from the number, and Surfline's words are its forecasters', not a lookup.
  • Interaction notes: haptics on iOS work only through the hidden switch-input trick (iOS 17.4+); one library says iOS 26.5 closed it, unverified, so treat it as an extra. NN/g: bottom sheets must dismiss with Back.
