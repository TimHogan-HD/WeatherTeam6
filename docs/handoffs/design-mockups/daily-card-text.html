Small text

A · right column

  • The score sits at the right edge of every day, right-aligned, so the seven scores make one column you read top to bottom. NN/g: for items to compare, a list with figures that line up, not cards.
  • The eye starts at the day on the left and ends at its answer on the right, like a table row.
  • Chance of rain beside it is small and grey, so the score is the one big number.

D · right, second line

  • Still in the right column so the scores line up, but on the line under the day instead of centred across both, and a size smaller. Chance of rain sits above it.

B · first reading

  • The score becomes the first labelled reading on the second line: Score 100, then Good, Rock, Sun. It follows the readings rule to the letter: a label and a value.
  • Quietest of the three. The score is no longer the biggest thing on the row, so picking a day takes a second look.

C · bar

  • A 0–100 bar per day, the number at its end. Length is the easiest visual to compare; the temperature bar becomes text so there is one bar per day.
  • Risk: with three 100s or near it the bars look alike. A saturated scale cannot rank, the bar just shows it.

Spacing, in all three

  • One row of three sizes, not six: the score (30), the day (20), everything else (14). NN/g: no more than three sizes in a group.
  • Day name and date share one line, so the top line is one height and the row is shorter. Day names are short (Wed, Thu) and sit in one fixed column, so the temperature bar stretches into the space that was empty and every day's bar spans the same width: one scale down the list.
  • 4 px between the lines inside a day, 16 px above and below each day plus the hairline. The space between days is now clearly larger than inside one, so each day reads as a group.
  • No sky icon; no wind, rain amount or model dots (owner, 2026-11-24).
  • Quieter, in every option: the score is the only colour on a row. Chance of rain is plain at the temperatures' size, 0% in grey. Sun is hours only; its times are on Hourly.
